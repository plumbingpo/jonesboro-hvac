When Your System Fails in Little Rock

When your heat or air quits, how fast help shows up matters more than anything else. For that reason, we keep scheduling flexible across the area and put a real person on the phone. Urgent calls usually get a technician within a day, and we treat no-heat and no-cool calls as top priority.

Here's what we see most often when something breaks at home: warm air from the AC, a condenser that hums but won't start, a furnace that lights for a few seconds and quits, a breaker that keeps tripping, an evaporator coil that has iced over, a condensate drain overflowing into the furnace cabinet, or a thermostat that has gone blank. Nearly all of these trace back to a part we already carry: capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and common fittings. You'll see the price up front, and that's the price you pay.

A little caution before we arrive can prevent a much bigger repair. A frozen air conditioner should be shut off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw safely. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to kill power at the furnace switch and call us right away.

How Little Rock Homeowners Can Extend HVAC Life

Many mid-season breakdowns start with skipped maintenance, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Change your filter every one to three months, keep plants and shrubs at least two feet from the outdoor condenser, and hose off the condenser coil once a year in spring. Then book a professional tune-up twice a year, one for cooling and one for heating, so our technicians can catch small problems while they're still cheap to fix.

How old is your system? Its age tells you a lot. With regular service, most air conditioners make it to 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15. If your system is beyond that point and also shows rising bills, uneven temperatures, or frequent repairs, it's usually due for a planned replacement, and that's almost always cheaper and easier on everyone than an emergency replacement when the first heat wave hits.

The sooner you call about a comfort problem, the smaller the repair tends to be. A capacitor that's starting to fail costs little to replace, yet that same capacitor, neglected for a season, can ruin the compressor and turn a simple fix into a full system replacement. Save our number in your contacts today so help is one call away as soon as something seems off in your home. A quick call saves time, money, and sleepless nights.
